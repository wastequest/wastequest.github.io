## p13 — PET-FOOD ("Makanan kucing daripada sisa ikan") → `petfood-poster-1.jpg` (materials + steps 1–4), `petfood-poster-2.jpg` (steps 5–8 + results)
Bahan-bahan: Sisa ikan (283 g); Tepung (1 1/4 cup & 3 tbsp); Telur (1 biji); Minyak (1 tbsp); Taurine (1/2 tbsp).
Juga memerlukan: pengukus, oven, mesin pengisar, mesin penimbang, sudu, pisau dan lain-lain.
1. Kumpul dan cuci sisa ikan — ambil bahagian ikan seperti kepala, insang dan isi dalam; cuci dengan air untuk buang kotoran dan darah.
2. Kukus sisa ikan — kukus selama lebih kurang 1 jam.
3. Kisar sisa ikan — lepas kukus, kisar sampai jadi puri (boleh guna pengisar mudah alih).
4. Keringkan puri ikan — masukkan ke dalam ketuhar untuk buang air; keringkan pada suhu 117 °C selama 15–20 minit.
5. Campur bahan (menjadi doh sisa ikan) — 1 biji telur, 2 cawan tepung, 1 sudu kecil minyak masak dan 0.8 g taurine ke dalam puri sisa ikan kering.
6. Bentuk doh — leperkan dengan penggelek; tabur sedikit tepung supaya tak melekat; bentukkan saiz kecil guna acuan.
7. Bakar makanan kucing — 150 °C selama 20 minit.
8. Bungkus — boleh terus dimakan kucing, atau dibungkus dan disimpan.
Hasilnya: photos after steaming / after blending / final product; Day 1 vs Day 14 — "Produk yang stabil. Tiada bau busuk atau fungi terhasil di makanan selepas disimpan selama 14 hari." "Mudah & Murah!"

## p15 — SAY NO TO LITMUS → `litmus-poster-1.jpg`
"Explore the pH-tastic journey!": (1) Sample challenges: guess the type; (2) pH testing time (LCD hidden!); (3) Precision guess: what's the pH value?; (4) Reveal & small talk: why pH matters in waste disposal; (5) Final competition: Mix it right!
pH scale 1–14: ACID "taste SOUR, turn litmus paper RED"; NEUTRAL 7; BASE "taste BITTER, feel SLIPPERY, turn litmus paper BLUE". "Which one is frequently found in food? ACID or BASE."
Improper waste disposal, a hidden danger: harms aquatic life by altering water pH; damages soil and stops plants growing; kills good bacteria; reacts dangerously with other substances in pipes. What you can do: test waste before pouring it away; avoid pouring strong acids/bases into sinks or drains; dilute acidic/basic liquids with water only with an adult; educate others.
- **[fix] Unsafe:** "taste sour / taste bitter / feel slippery" removed — never taste or touch test liquids. Replaced by "look at the indicator colour".
- **[interpretation]** No recipe on the poster. labs.js makes "say no to (bought) litmus" concrete: a natural indicator from kitchen waste (purple-cabbage outer leaves or bunga telang), indicator paper from scrap paper, the poster's hidden-LCD pH-meter guessing game, and the "Mix it right" neutralisation challenge. Household-safe samples only (no bleach, no drain cleaner).

## p18 — ECO-ENZYME → `enzyme-poster-1.jpg` (cover, "Eco-Mixzyme"-style logo), `-2` (materials), `-3` (method)
Materials (eco-enzyme): fruit peels, brown sugar, water, container; (eco-enzyme soap): soap base, mould. Materials (automatic mixer): Arduino Nano, 360-degree servo, jumper wire, 5 V adapter, metal wire (whisk), sealant, pressure-relief valve.
Methodology: (1) ratio fruit peel : brown sugar : water = 3 kg : 1 kg : 10 kg, mix; (2) blending; (3) store 1–3 months; (4) filter; (5) eco-enzyme. Do's: leave some air space for fermentation. - **[fix]** Gas release is listed under "Don'ts" but is a **Do**; and once a week is too seldom at the start — open briefly every day in the first 1–2 weeks, then weekly (or use a pressure-relief valve as the group did). Plastic container only, never glass.

## p20 — COMPOSTING → `compost-poster-1.jpg` (worm composting), `-2` (bokashi bin sketch, dimensions in inches), `-3` (quick bokashi guide)
Worm composting basics. Phase 1 pre-composting (thermophilic): drive off excess heat, pathogens and ammonia before adding worms; 5–15 days; 40–60 °C; thermophilic bacteria dominate; turn every 2–3 days; moisture 50–60% (damp sponge); optional biochar or dry leaves; PSB (phosphate-solubilising bacteria) ~0.5–1%. Phase 2 vermicomposting (mesophilic): worms (e.g. *Eisenia fetida*) convert pre-composted material into vermicast; 30–60 days; 15–30 °C; layer thin batches; bedding coconut coir, shredded paper or aged manure; worm density 0.5–1 kg per 1–1.5 kg food waste/week; moisture 70–80% (wet sponge, not dripping); passive aeration. Optional phase 3 curing: rest 1–2 weeks after harvest.
Quick bokashi guide: collecting wastes from the café; properly collecting and disposing of waste in bins, "create a 1:3:2 NPK ratio"; put a liner under the surface of the bin; "correctly gathering and placing waste, fertilizer, and worms into the bin".
- **[fix]** "1:3:2 NPK ratio" is not something a school can measure or control when filling a bin — replaced with the practical browns : greens ≈ 2–3 : 1 by volume (for worm/aerobic compost).
- **[fix]** Bokashi (sealed, anaerobic, bokashi bran) and worm composting (aerobic, worms) are two different methods; the poster mixes them ("worms into the bokashi bin"). labs.js teaches them separately: bokashi first, then bury or feed the fermented scraps to the worm bin.
- **[fix]** For Malaysia's heat, tropical worms (*Eudrilus eugeniae*, African nightcrawler; optimum ≈ 25–30 °C) are a better choice than *Eisenia fetida*; keep bins shaded.
